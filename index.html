Research

Work in Progress

  • Optimal Public Procurement Allocation: Balancing Sectoral Productivity and Competition (pdf)

    With Basile Grassi and Manuel Lleonart-Anguix

    This paper analyzes the optimal allocation of public procurement contracts in an oligopolistic economy. We develop a model in which a welfare-maximizing government selects its procurement strategy given an exogenous level of real public expenditure. Awarding procurement contracts alters market dynamics by affecting the markups of winning firms. While allocating contracts to the most productive firms seems optimal when considered in isolation, this strategy may not maximize welfare once sectoral and aggregate interactions are taken into account. The optimal allocation depends on the relative elasticities of substitution within and across sectors, as well as on the within sector dispersion of firm productivities. Under empirically plausible conditions, a more balanced distribution of contracts—rather than concentrating them among the most efficient firms—can enhance welfare. These findings highlight the role of procurement policy in shaping market structure, firm-level markups, and overall economic performance.

  • Public Procurement and the Labor Mix

    This paper examines how public procurement shapes firms’ choices between temporary and permanent employment. When future demand is uncertain, firms may hesitate to commit to permanent jobs and rely instead on temporary workers. Government contracts that provide a predictable stream of work could make longer-term hiring more attractive and help temporary workers move into permanent positions. I explore whether the duration of these contracts allows public purchasing to act as a form of insurance against demand uncertainty, supporting more stable employment.
